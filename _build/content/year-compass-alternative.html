YearCompass alternative: an honest comparison

YearCompass is a very good free booklet. If you are looking for an alternative, it is probably because you want to do it online, or because you want something that is still with you in March. This page covers both.

The short answer

Want a printed booklet, in almost any language, at no cost? Use YearCompass. Want to answer in your browser, see your life as a wheel and check in every month? Use the free tools here. Plenty of people do both.

What YearCompass is

YearCompass is a free booklet that helps you close one year and plan the next. You download it, set aside a quiet few hours, and work through its questions and exercises.

Its own site says it has been used by more than a million people around the world since 2012. It is run by a small team and translated by volunteers.

What YearCompass does well

  • It is free. There is no charge and no catch.
  • It comes in more than 50 languages. Few tools of any kind can say that.
  • It works on paper. You can print it at A4, or as a small A5 booklet that folds. Writing by hand slows you down in a useful way.
  • It has a digital format too. You can fill the booklet in on a device if you would rather not print.
  • It has a long record. People have come back to it every December for over a decade.

If that is what you are after, you do not need an alternative. Go and download it.

Where it stops

YearCompass is a booklet you complete once, at the turn of the year. As far as we can see on its site in November 2026, that is the whole of it. Nothing asks you in April how the plan is going.

That is not a fault. It is what a booklet is. But it is the reason most people who search for an alternative are searching. They did the reflection, felt clear for a fortnight, and found the booklet in a drawer in June.

The other reason is format. Some people simply think better typing than writing, and want their answers saved where they can find them again.

Where YearCompass is better

It would be dishonest to leave this out.

  • Languages. We are English only.
  • Paper. Their booklet is designed to be printed and folded. Ours prints, but it was made for a screen.
  • Trust. They have fourteen years and a million users. We are new.
  • Depth of ritual. A few hours with a pen and a booklet is a different experience from an hour at a keyboard. Some people need that.

Where The Alignment Plan is better

  • It continues. The wheel of life saves each month and lays the new one over the old, so you can see which way you are moving.
  • It is quick to start. Five minutes on the wheel will tell you something true before you have committed an evening.
  • It turns into a plan. The yearly goals template takes one focus and up to three goals, and breaks each into a step for every month.
  • It is written for adults with full lives. The 45 reflection questions start ten years back, not just twelve months.

Which should you use?

Use YearCompass if you love paper, you want it in a language other than English, or you want a long, quiet evening with a pen.

Use the tools here if you would rather type, you want your answers saved, or you know from experience that your December plans fade by spring.

Use both if you already have a YearCompass habit. Keep the booklet for December. Then rate your wheel on the first of every month. The booklet tells you where you want to go. The monthly wheel tells you whether you are going there.
