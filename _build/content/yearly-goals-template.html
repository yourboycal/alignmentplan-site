Yearly goals template

One focus for the year, up to three goals, and a step for every month. Fill it in here, or print it blank and use a pen. It takes about fifteen minutes.

How to use this yearly goals template

  1. Write your focus first. If you cannot name what the year is about, you are not ready to set goals. The reflection questions will help.
  2. Add one goal. Make it something you can finish, with a month to finish it by.
  3. Work backwards. Start at the finish month and fill in the steps towards January.
  4. Add a second or third goal only if you need to. One finished goal beats three abandoned ones.
  5. Print it. A plan you can see on a wall gets more attention than one in a tab.

Why one focus and only three goals

Most yearly goal lists fail for the same reason. They are too long. Ten goals feel ambitious on the first of January, but each one has to compete with the other nine for the same evenings and weekends.

A single focus solves the choosing. When something new comes up in May, you ask one question: does this serve the focus? If not, you have your answer.

Three goals is a ceiling, not a target. The template marks the second and third as optional on purpose.

How to break a goal into monthly steps

Big goals stall because nobody knows what to do about them this week. Working backwards fixes that.

Take "launch to ten customers by September". Ten customers by September means the first paying customer by about June. That means a waitlist by May, conversations with likely customers in April, and a first version built in March. Which means a one-page offer in February, and the time blocked out in January.

Now January's step is "block Friday mornings". That is small enough to do, and it is plainly connected to the thing you want.

Rules for a good monthly step

  • You can finish it inside the month.
  • You would know for certain whether it was done.
  • It starts with a verb: write, book, build, call, save.
  • There is only one per goal per month.

What to do when a month goes wrong

One will. A step gets missed, and the usual reaction is to feel behind and quietly stop looking at the plan.

Do the opposite. On the first of the month, open the template and move things. A missed step either rolls into this month, or it was the wrong step and should be replaced. A plan that changes every month is working. A plan that has not been opened since January is not.
