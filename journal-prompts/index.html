New year journal prompts: 52, one for every week

Journal prompts for the new year, for self reflection and for self growth, written for adults. There is one for each week of 2027, so you never open a blank page. Write here in your browser, or print the list for your journal.

Prompt 1 of 52

What do you want to feel more of this year?

What you write stays in this browser. Nothing you type is sent to us.

Weeks 1 to 8

New year journal prompts

For January and February, while the year is still new and your plans are still soft enough to change.

  1. What do you want to feel more of this year?
  2. What are you bringing with you from last year, and what are you leaving behind?
  3. If this year had one job to do for you, what would it be?
  4. What would make this a good year even if nothing big happens?
  5. Which goal on your list really belongs to someone else?
  6. What will you have to say no to, to make room for what you want?
  7. What is the smallest step you could take this week towards the thing you want most?
  8. Which of your plans for the year is still alive, and why that one?
Weeks 9 to 20

Journal prompts for self reflection

For seeing yourself clearly. None of these has a right answer, and the first answer is rarely the whole one.

  1. What have you been pretending not to know?
  2. When did you last change your mind about something important?
  3. What do people thank you for most often?
  4. What do you do when nobody is watching that you are proud of?
  5. Where in your life are you going through the motions?
  6. Look at your calendar for the last month. What does it say you care about, and is it true?
  7. Which of your habits would you not want a child to copy?
  8. What compliment do you find hard to accept?
  9. Who are you at your best? Describe that person's ordinary Tuesday.
  10. What are you envious of, and what does that tell you about what you want?
  11. What story do you tell about yourself that might be out of date?
  12. What have you forgiven yourself for, and what are you still holding on to?
Weeks 21 to 32

Journal prompts for self growth

For the middle of the year, when the easy progress is behind you and the next step asks a bit more.

  1. What are you better at than you were a year ago?
  2. What would you attempt if you were allowed to be bad at it?
  3. Which piece of feedback stung because it was true?
  4. What conversation are you avoiding, and what is the first sentence of it?
  5. Where are you waiting for permission, and from whom?
  6. It is the middle of the year. What has moved since January, and what has not?
  7. Which one habit would make your other habits easier?
  8. What did you learn the hard way that you could now teach?
  9. Who is already where you want to be, and what do they do differently?
  10. What small annoyance could you fix in a single afternoon?
  11. What would the braver version of you do this month?
  12. What do you need to stop doing in order to grow?
Weeks 33 to 40

Journal prompts for when you feel stuck

For the weeks when nothing is moving. These are short on purpose. Answer one and stop.

  1. What exactly is the problem? Write it in one sentence.
  2. If a friend were in your position, what would you tell them?
  3. What are you afraid will happen if you choose?
  4. What is the worst realistic outcome, and could you live with it?
  5. What worked the last time you felt like this?
  6. What would this look like if it were easy?
  7. What is one thing you can do in the next ten minutes?
  8. What are you making more complicated than it needs to be?
Weeks 41 to 46

Gratitude journal prompts

For the autumn. Each one asks for something specific, because "family, health, home" stops meaning anything by the third time you write it.

  1. Who made this year easier for you, and do they know?
  2. What ordinary thing would you miss most if it were gone?
  3. What went right this week that you had nothing to do with?
  4. What has your body done for you lately that you took for granted?
  5. What did somebody teach you this year without meaning to?
  6. What do you have now that you once wished for?
Weeks 47 to 52

End of year journal prompts

For the last six weeks of the year. They close the year one piece at a time, so December is not left to do it all.

  1. What surprised you about this year?
  2. What do you want to remember about this year in ten years' time?
  3. Who did you become a little more like this year?
  4. What is unfinished that you are willing to let go of?
  5. What are you taking into next year that you did not have a year ago?
  6. Write a short letter to yourself, to open this time next year.

How to use these journal prompts

  1. One prompt a week is enough. The box at the top of the page shows the prompt for the current week. Come back each week and it will have moved on.
  2. Set a timer for ten minutes. Write until it goes off. Stopping while you still have something to say makes it easier to come back.
  3. Write the obvious answer first. Then write "and also" and keep going. The second answer is usually the honest one.
  4. Do not edit. Nobody is marking this. Spelling, grammar and neat handwriting are not the point.
  5. Skip a prompt that does nothing for you. Press "Another prompt". But notice the ones you want to skip because they are uncomfortable. Those are often the ones worth doing.

Paper or screen?

Use whichever you will keep up. Paper is slower, which gives you time to think, and a notebook has no notifications. A screen is faster and always with you.

If you write here, your words are saved in your own browser on this device, one answer for each prompt. If you prefer a notebook, press "Print all 52" and stick the list inside the front cover.

What to do when you do not know what to write

Start by copying the prompt out by hand or typing it again. Then write "I do not know, but if I had to guess" and finish the sentence. It sounds like a trick, and it is, but it works. Most of the time you do know. You are waiting for a better answer than the one you have.

If nothing comes, describe your day so far in three sentences and stop. Ten minutes with a pen still counts.

Journal prompts for the new year, in the right order

The new year prompts on this page look forward. They work better if you have looked back first. If you have an hour before January, the 45 end of year reflection questions take you through the last ten years, the last twelve months and where you are today.

When the prompts have helped you work out what you want, give it some structure. The yearly goals template turns it into one focus, up to three goals and a step for every month. The wheel of life is a five minute check on the first of each month.

Questions about journal prompts

What are good journal prompts for the new year?

Good new year prompts ask what you want the year to feel like and what you will give up to get it, not only what you want to achieve. For example: what do you want to feel more of this year, and what will you have to say no to? The first eight prompts on this page are written for January and February.

How often should I use a journal prompt?

Once a week is plenty. This page has 52 prompts so there is one for every week of the year. Ten minutes on one prompt, done every week, does more than an hour done once in January.

How long should I write for?

About ten minutes. Set a timer and stop when it goes off, even if you are mid-thought. Stopping with something left to say makes it easier to start next time.

Are these journal prompts for adults?

Yes. They are about work, money, relationships, habits and the choices adults make. They are not classroom prompts, although older teenagers could use most of them.

Is what I write private?

Yes. Anything you type is stored in your own browser on this device and is not sent to us or anyone else. Clearing your browsing data or using a private window removes it.

Can I print the journal prompts?

Yes. Press Print all 52 to print the full list, grouped by theme, or choose Save as PDF in the print window to keep it as a file.

What is the difference between journal prompts and reflection questions?

Reflection questions look back over a period, usually a year, in one long sitting. Journal prompts are for a short, regular habit. Use the reflection questions once in December and the prompts once a week after that.
