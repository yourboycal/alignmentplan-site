End of year reflection questions for adults

45 questions for an honest look at your year, written for adults and not for classrooms. They help you see where you are and what is missing. Answer here in your browser, at your own pace.

Part 1

The long view

Start far back. A year is easier to judge once you can see the ten around it.

  1. Ten years ago, what did you think your life would look like by now?

    The gap between that picture and today shows what changed: you, or the plan.

  2. Which decision from the last ten years shaped your life the most?

    Big turns are easier to see from a distance.

  3. What did you want badly five years ago that no longer matters to you?

    Wanting changes. That is a reason to look twice at what you want today.

  4. What have you kept doing for years, even when nobody asked you to?

    What you keep returning to is usually what you value.

  5. Which friendship or relationship has lasted the longest, and why has it?

    It tells you what you need from people.

  6. What is the hardest thing you have come through, and what did it leave you with?

    You are more durable than one bad month suggests.

  7. When in your life did you feel most like yourself?

    This is your reference point for a life that fits.

  8. What did you believe about work or money ten years ago that you have since dropped?

    Old rules often keep running long after you stop believing them.

  9. Which place you have lived or worked suited you best?

    Surroundings matter more than most of us admit.

  10. What skill have you built slowly that you now take for granted?

    Slow progress is invisible until you look back.

  11. Whose advice turned out to be right?

    It shows whose voice is worth listening for.

  12. What risk are you glad you took?

    Keep it as evidence for the next one.

  13. What risk are you glad you did not take?

    Caution deserves credit too.

  14. What have you been saying "one day" about for more than five years?

    A "one day" either needs a date or needs letting go.

  15. If you met yourself from ten years ago, what would surprise them most about you now?

    A quick way to see how far you have come.

Part 2

The last twelve months

Now the year itself. Go month by month in your head, or open your calendar and your photos to jog your memory.

  1. If this year were a chapter in a book, what would its title be?

    Naming the year makes you decide what it was about.

  2. Which three moments from this year would you keep?

    What you would keep shows what to make more of.

  3. What did you finish this year?

    Finished things are forgotten faster than unfinished ones.

  4. What did you start and quietly drop?

    No blame. Dropped things show where the plan or the timing was wrong.

  5. Where did most of your time actually go?

    Time is the most honest record of your priorities.

  6. Where did most of your money go, beyond the bills?

    The same question, in a different ledger.

  7. What gave you energy this year?

    This is what to protect next year.

  8. What drained you this year?

    Do less of it, or change how you do it.

  9. Who did you see more of this year, and who did you see less of?

    People drift in and out without anyone deciding.

  10. What was the best decision you made this year?

    Look at how you made it, so you can do it again.

  11. What would you do differently if you had the year again?

    Regret is useful once, as an instruction.

  12. What did you learn about yourself that you did not know in January?

    A year that taught you nothing has not been looked at closely enough.

  13. What were you worried about a year ago that turned out fine?

    It is a check on how well your worries predict anything.

  14. What did you say yes to that you should have turned down?

    This shows where your limits are soft.

  15. What are you proud of that nobody else noticed?

    Private wins count.

Part 3

Where you are today

The past is done. These questions are about this week and what is missing from it.

  1. How would you describe your life right now to a close friend, in three sentences?

    A plain summary is harder to hide behind than a long one.

  2. Which part of your life is working well at the moment?

    Start from what is solid.

  3. Which part of your life have you been neglecting?

    You usually already know.

  4. What is missing from your life right now?

    This is the question most people skip. Be specific.

  5. What are you putting up with?

    Small things you tolerate add up to a mood.

  6. What does an ordinary good day look like for you?

    Good years are built out of ordinary days.

  7. What do you do every week that you would like to stop?

    Stopping one thing frees more time than any new habit.

  8. What do you wish you had more time for?

    Write it down so you can see whether next year makes room for it.

  9. Who do you want to be closer to?

    A name is more useful here than a category.

  10. How is your health, honestly?

    Think about your body, your sleep and your mood.

  11. How do you feel about your work when you wake up on a Monday?

    The feeling tells you more than the job title.

  12. What are you avoiding thinking about?

    Naming it is half the work.

  13. What would you try next year if you knew nobody would judge you?

    It separates what you want from what looks sensible.

  14. If the next twelve months went well, what would be different by this time next year?

    This is the bridge from looking back to making a plan.

  15. What is the one change that would improve most of the rest?

    Your answer is a good candidate for next year's single focus.

How to use these questions

  1. Give it a proper hour. Phone in another room. This is a sit-down session, not something to do between emails.
  2. Write, do not just think. A thought can stay vague for years. A written sentence has to say something.
  3. Be specific. "I was stressed" tells you little. "I worked eleven Saturdays" tells you what to change.
  4. Do not fix anything yet. The job today is to see clearly. Plans come afterwards.
  5. Skip what does not apply. If a question has no answer for you, leave it. Come back to any question you want to avoid.

Why start ten years back

Most year-end reviews only look at the last twelve months. That makes one year carry too much weight. A hard year looks like a failure and a good one looks like luck.

Part 1 gives the year its context. When you can see the decisions and habits of a decade, this year becomes one step in a longer story, and it is much easier to judge fairly.

What to do with your answers

Read Part 3 again once you have finished. Three questions there do most of the work: what is missing, what you have been neglecting, and the one change that would improve the rest.

Next, turn the words into a picture. The wheel of life takes five minutes. You rate eight areas of your life from 1 to 10 and see at once where the gaps are.

If you would like the method behind the questions, how to reflect on your year sets it out in seven steps, with the research.

Then choose one focus for the year. One, not five. A reflection that ends in a long list of resolutions has usually been forgotten by February.

Questions about the reflection

How long do these reflection questions take?

About an hour for all 45 if you write a few lines for each. You can split them into three sittings, one for each part. Your answers are saved in your browser as you type, so you can stop and come back.

Are these reflection questions for adults or for students?

For adults. Most end of year reflection questions online are written for classrooms. These are about work, money, relationships, health and the choices you have made over ten years, so they suit anyone who has left school.

Are my answers private?

Yes. Your answers are stored in your own browser on this device and are not sent to us or anyone else. If you clear your browsing data or use a private window, they will be removed, so print them or save a PDF if you want to keep them.

Can I print the questions or save them as a PDF?

Yes. Press Print or save as PDF at the top of the questions. With no answers typed in, you get a blank set to fill in by hand. With answers, you get your full reflection.

When is the best time to do an end of year reflection?

Any quiet hour between late November and the middle of January. The week between Christmas and New Year is popular because work is quiet. The date matters less than giving it a full hour without your phone.

What should I do after answering the questions?

Look at your answers to Part 3 and pick the one thing that is missing or neglected. Then rate each area of your life with the wheel of life to see the gaps as a picture. Those two things give you a focus for next year.
